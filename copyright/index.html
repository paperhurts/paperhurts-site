How Paperhurts stores stories

Paperhurts Reader is a personal reading app. Readers bring their own copies of stories, as files they already have or from websites they read, and the app keeps each copy in that reader's own library so they can read and listen to it on their devices. Each copy belongs to one account. Libraries are private: we don't publish them, there are no public pages or shared collections, and one reader can't browse another reader's library.

Paperhurts doesn't sell stories, and we don't charge for access to any of them.

What readers may import

Import only works you have the right to read and keep a personal copy of. For example:

  • stories the author has published freely online, saved for your own reading (with a site's download option, a tool like FicHub or FanFicFare, or the app's own import);
  • your own writing;
  • ebooks and documents you bought or were given legitimately, without copy protection removed;
  • works in the public domain or under a licence that allows it.

Don't import:

  • pirated ebooks: commercially published books you didn't buy, copies from piracy or "shadow library" sites, or any copy you know was made or shared without the rights holder's permission;
  • works an author has asked readers not to download or keep, where you know that;
  • anything else you don't have the right to copy.

Everything you import is for your own personal, non-commercial reading. Don't share, republish or sell it. The full rules are in our terms.

If you wrote it: having your story removed

If you wrote a story and don't want it in Paperhurts, email support@paperhurts.dev with "Author removal" in the subject. You don't need a lawyer, and you can use your pen name.

Show us the story is yours under that pen name: for example, send a link to your author page where it's published, and add a short note there that mentions Paperhurts, which you can delete once we've replied. [Owner: confirm how an author shows a pen name is theirs.]

Once we're satisfied it's yours, we:

  • remove every copy in any Paperhurts library that came from the story's link or records it, including the copy the app keeps for offline reading, the next time that reader opens the app with a connection;
  • block it from being added again, from its link or from a downloaded copy that records its link;
  • tell each reader who had it which story was removed and why.

Our terms also forbid readers from adding a removed story back in any other way.

We never tell you, or any other reader, whether anyone had it. Our reply is the same either way.

Sending a takedown notice

If you own the copyright in a work, or are authorised to act for the owner, and you believe copies of it are stored on Paperhurts without permission, send a written notice to our designated agent (below). Under the U.S. Digital Millennium Copyright Act (17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(3)), the notice must include:

  1. your physical or electronic signature (typing your full name is fine);
  2. the work you say is being infringed;
  3. the material you want removed, with enough detail for us to find it (see below);
  4. your name, postal address, phone number and email address;
  5. a statement that you believe in good faith that the use isn't authorised by the copyright owner, its agent or the law;
  6. a statement that the information in the notice is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that you're the copyright owner or authorised to act for them.

Finding the material. Because libraries are private, you won't be able to point to a page on Paperhurts. Instead, tell us the work's title, author name, and where it was published. We find copies by the link to the original, so that link is the most useful thing you can send. We'll remove the copies stored from it, or recording it. We don't tell you whether we found any.

If you wrote the story, you don't need a formal notice: the simpler route in If you wrote it is enough.

Counter-notices

If something was removed from your library and you believe it was a mistake, or the material was misidentified, you can send a counter-notice to our agent. Under 17 U.S.C. § 512(g)(3) it must include:

  1. your physical or electronic signature;
  2. the material that was removed, and where it appeared before it was removed (the story's title in your library is enough);
  3. a statement, under penalty of perjury, that you believe in good faith the material was removed by mistake or misidentification;
  4. your name, postal address and phone number, and a statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the federal district court for the judicial district where your address is (or, if you're outside the United States, any judicial district in which we may be found), and that you'll accept service of process from the person who sent the original notice or their agent.

We forward a valid counter-notice to the person who sent the original notice. Unless they tell us within 10 business days that they've filed a court action, we restore the material 10 to 14 business days after we receive the counter-notice.

Repeat infringers

We close the accounts of readers who repeatedly infringe copyright.

  • Each valid notice against an account counts as one strike, unless it's withdrawn or the reader sends a counter-notice that isn't challenged in court.
  • An account with three strikes within 12 months is closed.
  • We may close an account after a single valid notice that covers many commercially published ebooks.
  • [Owner and lawyer: does an author removal count as a strike?]
  • Before we close an account, we give the reader 14 days to export the rest of their library, unless the law or a court order prevents it.
  • A reader whose account was closed under this policy may not open a new one.

False notices

Under 17 U.S.C. § 512(f), anyone who knowingly misrepresents that material is infringing, or that it was removed by mistake, can be liable for damages. If you're not sure whether something infringes your rights, consider getting advice before you send a notice.
